About the sport

Five disciplines. One complete athlete.

Modern pentathlon brings together swimming, running, shooting, fencing, and equestrian in one Olympic sport. Pierre de Coubertin, founder of the modern Olympic Games, is regarded as its founder after adapting the ancient pentathlon. The sport joined the Olympic programme in 1912.

“Tested a man’s moral qualities as much as his physical resources and skills, producing thereby the ideal, complete athlete.” Pierre de Coubertin

2012–2013

Joining the international movement

During the 2012 Olympic Games, the Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM) sought to extend support to Asian countries. Sri Lanka obtained full UIPM membership in 2013.

2014

Coaching and athlete development

Brazilian UIPM international coach Alexandre Franca led an awareness and coaching programme for eight coaches and 12 athletes in Sri Lanka. Coaches represented swimming, middle-distance running (400–800 metres), and shooting.

2015 snapshot

Taking the sport to schools

At the time, MPFSL fielded school teams rather than a national team, beginning with eight international schools in Colombo. The planned early competitions were Biathle (running and swimming), followed by Triathle (running, swimming, and shooting), with the first Biathle planned for May and a Triathle later that year. The federation also aimed to expand to zonal and national schools.

For Olympic Day on 23 June 2015 in Mannar, MPFSL was invited by the National Olympic Committee of Sri Lanka to organize a Biathle event, training, and introductory school workshops.

The pathway

From Biathle to the full pentathlon

The original development plan described Biathle and Triathle as early competition steps, with opportunities to progress to World Championships and, ultimately, the five-discipline Olympic event. Existing running and swimming facilities supported that start; more laser pistols were identified as a need for Triathle. The account also noted three UIPM Level 1 coaches and expressed confidence in growing the sport through commitment and support.

Gayani Dassanayake carries Sri Lanka’s pentathlon story to Nagoya

Asian Games · Modern Pentathlon

From Dematapelessa to Nagoya: a national champion on the Asian stage

Raised in Dematapelessa, Ampara, Gayani joined the Sri Lanka Air Force at 18 through her swimming ability and began competing as a full-time athlete with its aquatics team. She became National Triathlon Champion in 2016, then National Modern Pentathlon-Triathle Champion in 2019.

After winning the 2025 National Modern Pentathlon Championship in Bandaragama, she represented Sri Lanka at the 2025 World Cup in Egypt, the 2025 Asian Championships in Japan, and the 2026 World Championship in China. Her 2025 performances secured direct qualification for the 2026 Asian Games.

In Nagoya, Gayani placed 28th overall, ahead of competitors from Kuwait, Malaysia, Thailand, and Kyrgyzstan. She was the only woman representing South Asia in the modern pentathlon field, a milestone for Sri Lankan sport and women athletes.

International debut · 2–5 November 2023

Sri Lankan athletes make their mark in Bali

Sri Lankan athletes made their debut at the UIPM Biathle/Triathle World Championships in Bali, Indonesia, competing against experienced teams from around the world. Their performances marked an important step for the sport in Sri Lanka, just six years after its introduction.

Results in Bali

  • Arusha Hettiarachchi, Under 19: fourth in Triathle and fifth in Biathle. He finished the Triathle just nine seconds short of bronze.
  • Oshada Isiwaruna De Silva, Senior Men: eighth in Triathle and seventh in Biathle.
  • Ruwansiri Silva, Senior: 13th in Triathle and ninth in Biathle.
  • Team leader: former athlete and modern pentathlon coach Ms. Piumi Krishanthi.

The team’s debut showed the progress made in building an international pathway for Sri Lankan athletes and laid a foundation for the federation’s continued development of the sport.

Coaching · 23–27 January 2023

UIPM CCP Level 1 coaching programme expands Sri Lanka’s coaching base

The Modern Pentathlon Federation of Sri Lanka successfully conducted a UIPM Coaches Certification Programme (CCP) Level 1 course from 23 to 27 January 2023. UIPM’s Ms. Carina Vicente (Portugal) led the programme, which brought together 15 coaches from across the country.

Programme partners

  • UIPM: main sponsor and international programme partner.
  • Cinnamon Hotels & Resorts: official hotel partner.
  • Department of Sports Development: co-sponsor.

MPFSL’s vision was to bring more coaches into modern pentathlon and build a strong coaching base for the next generation of athletes. The federation thanked all stakeholders for supporting the development of the sport in Sri Lanka.
